# AI Model-Release / Flagship Timing Markets

Use this workflow for markets like `Will a new Gemini/OpenAI/Claude flagship be released by <date>?`

## Rule-first checklist

Read the rule text before tech news. These markets often hinge on exact words:

- `released`
- `made available`
- `general public`
- `GA`
- `preview`
- `reasoning-focused`
- `flagship`
- `Pro`
- `Deep Think`
- `Ultra`
- exclusions for Flash, Lite, Nano, TTS, embedding, robotics, API tooling, or agent features

Separate:

- `P(any model/product update)`
- `P(qualifying flagship)`
- `P(public availability by deadline)`
- `P(oracle/admin/UMA interprets it as qualifying)`

## Sibling ladder

Build the full sibling time ladder, such as `by May 15`, `by May 22`, `by May 31`, `by Jun 30`. A later bucket near 95% while the near bucket is 50-60% usually means direction is likely but timing is concentrated around one catalyst window.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Official launch blogs, model cards, API/model docs, changelogs, and availability pages.
2. Official conference schedules and keynote pages.
3. High-quality tech reporting.
4. Search snippets and X posts for discovery only.

Classify each news item:

- qualifies or likely qualifies under the rule
- directionally relevant but not enough
- explicitly non-qualifying

## Market-move diagnosis

For price moves, inspect CLOB history and recent trades. A fast move may be hard news, missing expected catalyst, rule reinterpretation, adjacent-bucket repricing, or thin-order-book flow.

Always distinguish announcement risk from availability risk. A keynote demo or trusted-tester preview may move price without resolving Yes.

## Sizing

Cap single-event product-release exposure below mechanical Kelly when the edge depends on product naming, public-availability interpretation, or admin/UMA discretion.
